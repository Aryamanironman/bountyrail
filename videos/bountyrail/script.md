# BountyRail: bounties that pay themselves

## problem {hold=1.1}
Open source bounties usually begin as a promise. [#issue] A company offers money in an issue. [#merge] The work gets merged. [#wait] Then the contributor waits for weeks. Some payments never arrive.

## flip {hold=0.9}
BountyRail changes the order. [#lock] The sponsor puts the money in first. [#held] PayPal holds the full amount before anyone writes code.

## post {hold=0.9}
A bounty lists its acceptance criteria and the contributor's PayPal email. [#approve] The sponsor approves one authorization. [#captured] The funds are held, not captured.

## review {hold=1.1}
When a pull request arrives, an AI judge reads the patch and its tests. [#checks] It checks every criterion and scores the work. [#score] Each score comes with evidence.

## human {hold=0.9}
The judge has no power to move money. [#key] A maintainer reads the evidence and confirms. [#second] That confirmation is the second key.

## pay {hold=1.2}
PayPal captures the authorization and pays the contributor. [#ids] The order, the capture and the payout each leave an ID on the bounty.

## close {hold=1.8}
The money is there before the work. It moves when the work is verified.
